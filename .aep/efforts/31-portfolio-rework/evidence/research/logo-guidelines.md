# Question

For each of eight technology logos drawn by Simple Icons (npm package simple-icons 16.32.0, one monochrome SVG path per mark), do the owner's published brand or trademark guidelines allow a personal portfolio website to show the logo (a) as a small reference mark beside the technology's name, in a badge naming a tool the developer used on a project card or skill card, and (b) drawn in a single colour, the page's text colour (`currentColor`), in both light and dark themes? The eight are TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, Node.js, CSS, PostgreSQL, npm, Rust and Godot Engine.

All pages were read on 2026-09-24 with a plain HTTP fetch (following redirects), converted to text, and searched by hand. Linked PDFs were read with pdftotext. Official logo files were downloaded and their SVG fill colours listed; the eight Simple Icons paths from the installed package (`node_modules/simple-icons`, version 16.32.0 confirmed from its `package.json`) were rendered to an image and compared by eye with the official files. Web search was used only to find the Godot trademark policy.

Scope: this file reports what each owner's guidelines say. It is not legal advice, and it does not weigh trademark law's own nominative fair use doctrine, which can allow uses a policy does not list.

# Findings

Each finding is labelled source (what a page says, quoted), observation (what I saw in files), interpretation (what I infer), or verdict (the classification the brief asked for, which is itself an interpretation).

## 1. TypeScript (L1)

- F1. source, reference use: the page lists "Please Don't" items: "Use the TypeScript logos for your application/product.", "Modify the shape of the logos when used.", "Integrate the TypeScript logo into your application's logo.", "Name a product which implies TypeScript's endorsement of the product." It says the blue mark "is the right logo for blog posts, news articles, giveaway stickers and general marketing for yourself." Nothing on the page addresses portfolios or badges directly.
- F2. source, colour: under "Alternatives": "Single color variant which has the "TS" cut out, useful for when you need a single color design." The primary mark note reads: "The "TS" in the logo is white, not transparent by default."
- F3. observation: the page's image of the single-colour variant (`two-colors.svg`, alt text "Examples of the logos as single colors") shows the same cut-out mark twice, white on a blue field and black on a white field, so the variant is shown in more than one colour. The downloadable asset pack contains the blue logo, a round version, and blue and white lettermarks, but no separate single-colour cut-out file. The Simple Icons path is that cut-out shape (a rounded square with "TS" knocked out).
- F4. attribution: none asked for.
- F5. verdict: usable as a monochrome reference mark. The owner offers a single-colour cut-out variant for single-colour designs and shows it in two different colours; the only shape rule is not to modify the shape; a badge naming the tool is not using the logo for your own product or implying endorsement. Confidence: high.

## 2. Tailwind CSS (L2)

- F6. source, reference use: "The Tailwind name and logos are trademarks of Tailwind Labs Inc." "You may not use the Tailwind name or logos in any way that could mistakenly imply any official connection with or endorsement of Tailwind Labs Inc. Any use of the Tailwind name or logos in a manner that could cause customer confusion is not permitted." And: "These assets are provided for use in situations like articles and video tutorials. They should not be used in any way that could be confusing for customers or imply any affiliation with Tailwind Labs Inc." The other restrictions are about product and domain names ("Tailwind Themes", "tailwindkits.com") and merchandise.
- F7. source, colour: the page says nothing about colour, recolouring, monochrome use or altering the mark. I searched the page text for colour, alter and modify and found nothing relevant.
- F8. observation: the mark is offered in one file only, sky blue (`#38bdf8`). The logotype zip has a dark-text and a white-text version, and both keep the sky-blue mark. No official one-colour or monochrome mark is offered.
- F9. attribution: none asked for.
- F10. verdict: usable as a monochrome reference mark, with medium confidence. The guidelines permit use that does not imply affiliation, give "articles and video tutorials" as examples rather than a closed list, and are silent on colour, so they neither permit nor forbid recolouring. No official monochrome variant exists, so the single-colour rendering is the Simple Icons redraw, not an owner-supplied file.

## 3. Node.js (L3, L4, L5)

- F11. source: the Node.js page says only: "Please review the trademark policy for information about permissible use of Node.js(R) logos and marks." and links L4. Its footer: "The OpenJS Foundation has registered trademarks and uses trademarks. For a list of trademarks of the OpenJS Foundation, please see our Trademark Policy and Trademark List."
- F12. source (L5): the word mark "Node.js" is listed among registered trademarks, and "Node.js Logo" among logo marks with registrations pending or in use (TM). True of the list dated 2022-02-14.
- F13. source (L4), colour: under "Rules that Apply to OpenJS Trademarks": "A trademark must not be altered or amended in any way. ... A logo must not be displayed with color variations, or with other elements superimposed on top of the logo." The example page labels one logo "Incorrect (must not change colors)". And: "The link and all other usage of a logo of the OpenJS Foundation shall be done using the official versions of the OpenJS Foundation logos obtained from the OpenJS Foundation or its projects."
- F14. source (L4), reference use: "Do not use a logo of the OpenJS Foundation on posters, brochures, signs, websites, crowdfunding sites, or other marketing materials to promote your events, products or services without written permission from the OpenJS Foundation." Also: "You are also allowed to use a trademark or logo of the OpenJS Foundation as a link to the home page of the applicable project or to a web page on the OpenJS Foundation web site that is relevant to the reference so long as the link is in a manner that is consistent with the preservation of the goodwill and value of the mark." Fair use is described for word marks only: "You may make fair use of word marks to make true factual statements."
- F15. source (L4), notices: "A trademark notice must be used on the most prominent and/or first appearance of each mark of the OpenJS Foundation". "Even when making fair use of a trademark, you must acknowledge the owner of the trademark with a trademark notice, such as the notice displayed on the OpenJS Foundation project websites." "Any use of registered trademarks listed above should include the (R) symbol immediately after the first usage".
- F16. observation: the branding page offers, among others, "JS Icons" in two files: `jsIconGreen.svg` (outline hexagon and "JS" in `#5FA04E`) and `jsIconWhite.svg` (the same drawing, all white). The Simple Icons path is this "JS" hexagon icon. So an official one-colour version exists, in white only.
- F17. interpretation: drawing the icon in the page's text colour produces a dark icon in the light theme, which is not an official version and is a colour variation. In the dark theme it matches the official white file only if the text colour is pure white. Separately, whether a personal portfolio "promote[s] your ... services" is a judgement the policy does not settle; the link permission in F14 covers a logo that links to nodejs.org and is an official version.
- F18. attribution: the (R) symbol after first use of "Node.js", and a trademark notice acknowledging the OpenJS Foundation (the nodejs.org footer wording in F11 is the model the policy points to).
- F19. verdict: usable only in brand colours, meaning the official green file, or the official white file on a dark background, and not recoloured to `currentColor`. Even the brand-colour use on a portfolio sits close to the "websites ... to promote your ... services" clause, which asks for written permission; the clearest permitted form is an official logo that links to nodejs.org. Confidence: high that recolouring is not permitted; medium on whether the badge needs permission at all.

## 4. CSS (L6)

- F20. source, licence: "The logo is released under Creative Commons Zero (CC0) Universal v1.0 ... Nevertheless, the CSS-Next Community Group decided to provide usage guidelines to make sure the logo is used in an accessible and recognizable manner." No trademark claim is made anywhere in the README.
- F21. source, colour: three official variants: main (rebeccapurple background, white text), dark ("black #000000 as the background color and white #FFFFFF as the font color. This logo should only be used on light backgrounds"), and light ("white #FFFFFF as the background color and black #000000 as the font color. This logo should only be used on dark backgrounds"). Under "Modifications", "Do's": "Change colors and background while maintaining a proper contrast ratio and legibility" and "Rotate and scale the logo as you need". "Don'ts": "Do not distort the logo by stretching or squashing it", "Do not add any effects such as shadows, gradients, or outlines to the logo", "Do not use different font families for enumeration or claim".
- F22. source, size and files: "The minimum size of the logo should be 40px in height for digital applications ... Exceptions are favicons or icons for example to visualize the file type in an IDE." And under "Usage on the web": "When embedding the official CSS logo in websites, always use the official image files provided by the CSS Next community group."
- F23. observation: the Simple Icons entry records its source as `css.svg` in this repository at a pinned commit (L14), and its path is that square with "CSS" knocked out.
- F24. interpretation: the "Do's" permit a colour change outright, so `currentColor` is within the guidelines provided contrast holds. A badge-sized mark is below 40px; the exception names icons, "for example" a file-type icon, which a badge resembles but is not named. The "official image files" sentence and the Simple Icons redraw pull slightly against each other, though the redraw is taken from the official file and the logo is CC0.
- F25. attribution: none asked for.
- F26. verdict: usable as a monochrome reference mark. Confidence: high on colour; medium on the minimum-size rule, which the icon exception probably but not certainly covers.

## 5. PostgreSQL (L7)

- F27. source, reference use: "the terms Postgres and PostgreSQL and the Elephant Logo (Slonik) are all registered trademarks of the PostgreSQL Community Association of Canada. If you wish to use these marks, you must comply with this policy." The "Fair Uses That Don't Require Prior Approval" section speaks of the names only: "You may make fair use of Postgres or PostgreSQL to refer to the software in true factual statements", with examples about software, events, articles, books, contributors, and "You may use Postgres or PostgreSQL on your website as links to our website". No listed fair use mentions the logo. "For pretty much any use not discussed above, you must contact us for permission before you use our marks."
- F28. source, form and colour: under "How To Use Our Marks": "The marks should not be modified in any way without prior approval. They should not be presented with other trademarks or logos." "The Elephant Logo (Slonik) must appear in one of the forms below:" followed by two images.
- F29. observation: the two permitted forms are `elephant.png` (the blue elephant with dark outline and white highlights) and `elephant-bw.png` (a black single-line outline drawing on white). The Simple Icons path is a different drawing: a filled silhouette following the double outline of the colour logo. It matches neither permitted form.
- F30. source, attribution: "we encourage you to present the marks with Postgres, PostgreSQL or the Elephant Logo (Slonik) followed by the (R) symbol wherever practicable. If you use the outline version of Slonik, this should be followed by the TM symbol." "Wherever possible, include the line: Postgres, PostgreSQL and the Slonik Logo are trademarks or registered trademarks of the PostgreSQL Community Association of Canada, and used with their permission."
- F31. interpretation: three separate points go against the badge: the logo's use is not among the listed fair uses (so it needs approval), the Simple Icons drawing is not one of the two forms the policy requires, and a row of technology badges presents Slonik "with other trademarks or logos". The black outline form is a one-colour form, but recolouring it for the dark theme would be a modification.
- F32. verdict: not usable without the Association's approval. The name as a plain text chip is a listed fair use. Confidence: high.

## 6. npm (L8)

- F33. source, logos: "the npm logos represent only npm and should not be used to represent your products. The npm Logos signify us, or a special relationship with us, and you may use them only with our permission." And: "Please be advised that the Wombat and the logos generally may not be used to refer to the project, service, or company in a nominative sense, as any usage will almost always imply a special relationship with npm."
- F34. source, name: "'Nominative' or 'referential' use means to refer to something or someone else by their trademark. So it's perfectly OK to use "npm" to refer to npm, Inc., the npm client, npm code, and the npm public registry." "When referring to the npm software in body text, the first usage should be followed by a generic term such as "package manager," "services" or "client" to provide context." "npm" should never be explained as an acronym.
- F35. source, colour: silent. No colour rule and no logo files are offered on the page, since logo use requires permission.
- F36. source, attribution: "Any materials referring to npm should include the following notice in the footer or wherever you may have your own trademark notice: "npm is a registered trademark of npm, Inc."" This applies to the name, so it applies to a text chip too.
- F37. verdict: not usable. The policy rules out nominative use of the logo specifically. The name is usable, with the footer notice. Confidence: high.

## 7. Rust (L9, L10, L11)

- F38. source, licence: "The Rust and Cargo logos (bitmap and vector) are owned by the Rust Foundation and distributed under the terms of the Creative Commons Attribution license (CC-BY). ... The restrictions are that distributors must "give appropriate credit, provide a link to the license, and indicate if changes were made." Note that use of these logos, and the Rust and Cargo names, is also governed by trademark". L11 says the same and links creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/.
- F39. observation: Simple Icons records the Rust icon's licence as CC-BY-SA-4.0 (L14). The owner's current pages (L9, L11) say CC BY and link version 4.0, not BY-SA. The footer credit planned under CC BY-SA 4.0 does not match the owner's own statement.
- F40. source, reference use: "The most basic rule is that the Rust trademarks cannot be used in ways that appear (to a casual observer) official, affiliated, or endorsed by the Rust Project or Rust Foundation, unless you have written permission from the Rust Foundation. ... This is a requirement for all uses". Among "Uses That Do Not Require Explicit Approval": "Stating accurately that software is written in the Rust programming language ... you may use the word "Rust" to indicate this, without prior approval." "Using unmodified Rust trademarks in books, blogs or publications like "Rust Journal" or "Rust Cookbook" is allowed." "Using the word "Rust" on websites, brochures, documentation, academic papers, books, and product packaging to refer to the Rust programming language or the Rust Project is allowed." "Modifications must be clearly disclosed alongside the distribution if they do not have prior approval." L11 summarises: "Most non-commercial uses of the Rust/Cargo names and logos are allowed and do not require permission; most commercial uses require permission. In either case, the most important rule is that uses of the trademarks cannot appear official or imply any endorsement by the Rust project."
- F41. source, colour: the trademark policy is silent on colour. L10's colour and "Never ... alter" rules apply to "the Rust Foundation logo", not the language logo. L11 describes official variants: "single-path" SVGs "use only a single `<path>` element and no advanced svg features", and "The `white-outline` version has a white background and outline, to make it usable on dark backgrounds".
- F42. observation: the official `rust-logo.svg` is black (no fill set on the main path). The official single-path variant is a one-colour form, and the Simple Icons path is the same gear and "R" drawing. The official answer for dark backgrounds is the black logo with a white outline and background, not a light-coloured logo.
- F43. interpretation: in the light theme a dark `currentColor` is close to or the same as the official black. In the dark theme a light `currentColor` is a colour change: the CC BY licence permits it provided changes are indicated, and the trademark policy's explicit logo permission covers "unmodified" marks in publications, while the artwork README says most non-commercial logo uses need no permission. A personal portfolio is not plainly commercial or non-commercial.
- F44. attribution: CC BY credit with a link to the licence and a note that the logo was changed (recoloured) where it is shown in a colour other than black.
- F45. verdict: usable as a monochrome reference mark, with medium confidence. The logo is itself one colour and the licence allows modification with notice; the dark-theme recolour is the uncertain part, and "logo recoloured" belongs in the credit.

## 8. Godot Engine (L12, L13)

- F46. source, press kit: "Godot logos are licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International." "Only use the logo and icon to represent Godot, not your own project. You are allowed to include the Godot logo in your project's splash screen, credits or website, but it should not be done in a way that implies endorsement of your project by the Godot developers." "Do not distort the logo or icon. In other words, always preserve the aspect ratio when scaling images."
- F47. source, colour: the press kit offers the icon as "Colored", "Colored with outline", "Monochrome (for light backgrounds)" and "Monochrome (for dark backgrounds)", and the same monochrome pair for each logo layout. Observation: the monochrome icon files are single-fill SVGs, near-black (`#010102`) and white (`#fff`). The Simple Icons path is this icon.
- F48. source (L13), reference use: the licence is granted to "any person or legal entity using the GODOT Software ... for the sole purpose of conveying the message that it is using the GODOT Software (e.g. a game or app "made with GODOT ENGINE")". "The GODOT logo can be used to make references to the GODOT ENGINE or the GODOT Project, in contexts that are allowed under this Policy and License." The logo may not be used "as the logo for any website, project, service, initiative, company". "It must be clear at all times that you and your use of the Godot Trademarks and the context in which you use the trademarks is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Godot Foundation without the latter's prior written consent". The policy is silent on colour and even allows "a derivative of the logo that fits your game for the splash screen".
- F49. source (L13), attribution: a recommended, not required, disclaimer: ""<Your brand/company name> is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Godot Foundation. <Your brand/company name> uses the GODOT(R) name and logos under a permissive license granted by the Godot Foundation". Inclusion of this statement is not a hard requirement". Also: "you cannot use these trademarks in isolation", meaning there must be context saying what they are used for.
- F50. interpretation: a project card saying the project was built with Godot fits the first licensed purpose closely. A skill card is further from "conveying the message that it is using the GODOT Software", but still refers to the engine the developer uses.
- F51. verdict: usable as a monochrome reference mark. Official monochrome variants exist; the only visual rule is no distortion; the trademark policy permits references and derivatives. Confidence: high for project cards, medium to high for skill cards.

# Conclusion

- conclusion: of the eight, five are usable as single-colour reference marks under their owners' guidelines: TypeScript and CSS explicitly (a single-colour variant; "Change colors" as a "Do"), Godot with official monochrome files and a permissive policy, Tailwind CSS because its page is silent on colour, and Rust with a caveat on the dark-theme recolour. Node.js is usable only as an official file (green, or white on dark), not recoloured, and even that sits near a clause asking for permission on promotional websites. PostgreSQL and npm are not usable as logos without the owner's permission; both names are usable as text.
- conclusion: notices the guidelines ask for, independent of the logo decision: npm asks for "npm is a registered trademark of npm, Inc." in any material that refers to npm; OpenJS asks for (R) after the first "Node.js" and a trademark notice; PostgreSQL encourages (R) and its attribution line "wherever possible". Godot recommends, but does not require, a non-affiliation disclaimer. Rust's logo licence is CC BY 4.0 per the owner, not the CC BY-SA 4.0 that Simple Icons records.
- Confidence: high on npm, PostgreSQL, TypeScript, CSS and the Node.js colour rule, where the text is direct; medium on Tailwind CSS and Rust, where the verdict rests on silence or on scope; medium on whether the Node.js promotional-website clause reaches a personal portfolio.
